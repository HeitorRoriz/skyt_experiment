\renewcommand{\shorttitle}{\sameeval{}: Is Regenerated Code the Same Program?}

\begin{abstract}
Code-generation benchmarks ask whether a program passes tests.
They do not ask whether the next passing program is the same program.
That distinction matters where review and change control are expensive.

\sameeval{} is an overlay on an existing task set.
\sameat{} is a U-statistic of order 2 with failures kept in the denominator;
\sameatc{} asks the same question among certified pairs and is secondary.

On 26{,}240 generations of all 164 HumanEval+ tasks from four models at two
temperatures, Plus pass is 86.4\% and \sameat{} is 67.7\%.
A fifth model at provider-default sampling reaches 92.9\% and 66.3\%.
GPT-4o-mini at $T{=}0.7$ reaches 79.7\% Plus pass and 48.9\% \sameat{},
the lowest \sameat{} among T-grid cells.
Tighter equivalence rulers widen that overlay gap.
Repeatability is not a proxy for hidden-test correctness
(exploratory): generation can be repeatably wrong, and form diversity does
not signal Extra-test failure within a task.
The expanded-pool replication is reported in Section~\ref{sec:rq3}.

On held-out generations of the two original models, with only HumanEval base
tests used during repair and Extra tests reserved for evaluation, \skyt{}
raises \sameat{} by 17.0\,pp (14.5--19.6) while Plus pass rises by 2.7\,pp.
A stored-program cache remains the ceiling on static prompts.
The gains are largest at $T{=}0.7$.

Repeatability is a separate dimension and should be measured explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Correctness benchmarks for LLM code generation---HumanEval~\cite{chen2021evaluating},
MBPP~\cite{austin2021program}, SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench}---ask whether
\emph{a} generated artifact passes tests.
Production pipelines regenerate: CI retries a generator, an agent re-samples
after a failed test, and two developers issue the same prompt.
Tests cannot tell two passing regenerations apart, yet the artifacts can
differ in structure~\cite{ouyang2023llm}.

This paper's primary contribution is \textbf{\sameeval{}}, a benchmark overlay
that measures that scatter.
It ships no new prompts and no new tests.
It adds a form report on top of an existing task set.
The report's headline is \sameat{}: the probability that two independent draws
both certify and are the same program.
A second column, \sameatc{}, asks the same identity question among passers
only; configs with fewer than two passers are dropped from that column and the
drop is reported.

\sameeval{} does not pick a canon and does not aim at a shipped human solution.
``Same form'' means identical canonical-form fingerprints
(Section~\ref{sec:sameness}).
Which verified form later generations should be repaired toward is a
\emph{policy}, not a measurement.
This paper measures scatter with \sameeval{} and then scores \skyt{} applied
to held-out generations.

We apply \sameeval{} to all 164 HumanEval+~\cite{liu2023evalplus} tasks at
protocol $N{=}20$ (Table~\ref{tab:he-table1}): four models at two temperatures
plus one at provider-default sampling (29{,}520 generations / 1{,}476 configs).
We then ask four questions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{RQ1.}
        How far apart are functional correctness and repeatability on
        HumanEval+?
  \item \textbf{RQ2.}
        Does \sameat{} measure what it claims, and does it tell us something
        pass@$k$ does not?
  \item \textbf{RQ3.}
        Does repeatability carry information about hidden-test correctness?
  \item \textbf{RQ4.}
        Can \sameeval{} separate mitigation policies, and where do their
        effects come from?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \sameeval{}: the \sameat{} / \sameatc{} estimators, with failures in
        the denominator, and a versioned canonical-form fingerprint.
  \item Validity evidence in this draft: three computational rulers, a rank
        comparison against pass rate, and sample-size reliability.
        Human agreement is still pending.
        Human agreement, rank reversal, and sample-size reliability are not
        yet reported.
  \item Empirical findings: correctness does not imply repeatability;
        generation can be repeatably wrong, so \sameat{} is not a proxy for
        hidden-test correctness (exploratory).
  \item A held-out, oracle-split protocol for evaluating mitigation policies,
        applied to raw generation, stored-program caching, \skyt{}, and a
        level ablation.
  \item Artifact: all generations, the scorer, and recompute commands.
\end{enumerate}

Ouyang \etal{}~\cite{ouyang2023llm} document non-determinism in code
generation.
\sameeval{} contributes a standardized estimator, certification conditioning,
a validated fingerprint, and a protocol for evaluating mitigations.

\paragraph{Not in this paper.}
Generation-by-construction (``Code DNA'') is a separate vision paper.
We do not score by pass@$k$ or by match to the HumanEval canonical solution.
We do not treat copying the first passer as the \skyt{} policy; we \emph{do}
report it as a cache baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
This study is Python HumanEval+, not an embedded or safety-certification
evaluation.
A regeneration track under perturbed contexts is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Outline.}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Regeneration is how LLM-assisted development actually works.
Correctness benchmarks record whether \emph{a} sample
passes~\cite{chen2021evaluating,liu2023evalplus}.
They do not record whether the next passing sample is the same program.
Two shortcuts look as if they dissolve that question.

\paragraph{Why not copy the first passer?}
That is a policy for what to ship, not a measurement of whether the next
draw is the same program.
The first passer is one order statistic of the sample.
Keeping it throws the other draws away.
Those draws are how scatter is estimated.
They are also, separately, the raw material for a later merge into a
stored form: a program that fails the suite can still carry a better
algorithm or a faster block than the mode.
This overlay does not perform that merge.
Unconstrained diversity in a \emph{shipped} artifact is still review cost,
which is why a team may eventually freeze \emph{a} form.
The design choice is which form, and whether later draws match it;
``whatever passed first'' skips both.
We still measure first-passer \emph{caching} as a baseline, because on a
static prompt it is a legitimate policy (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
Caching eliminates regeneration; \skyt{} constrains regeneration.

\paragraph{Why not stop calling the model after the first passer?}
That estimates pass@$1$.
\sameat{} is a pairwise rate; it is unidentified if sampling stops at one
success.
In the lab, blocking further calls hides the distribution rather than
showing it is tight.
In a pipeline the shortcut is narrower than it sounds.
Agents are not a one-shot sampler on a frozen prompt: they re-issue
prompts across retries, refactors, new files, other developers, and model
updates.
Caching one $(\mathrm{prompt},\mathrm{artifact})$ pair is possible, and it
is exactly copy-the-first-passer again.
Disabling the language model after a single green test is not how those
systems run, and it is not a measurement protocol.

Commercial APIs are not bit-deterministic at
$T{=}0$~\cite{ouyang2023llm}.
The test suite treats two passers as interchangeable; a reviewer who has to
read the second sample does not.
That is the measurement problem \sameeval{} is for.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Functional correctness.}
HumanEval~\cite{chen2021evaluating}, MBPP~\cite{austin2021program}, and
SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} score pass@$k$ against tests.
HumanEval+~\cite{liu2023evalplus} keeps the same 164 prompts and replaces the
hidden tests with a larger extra suite.
These protocols do not report how often independent draws share structure.
AlphaCode~\cite{li2022alphacode} clusters programs by input--output behavior to
filter duplicate submissions; that is a selection policy, not a population
report.

\paragraph{LLM variability.}
Ouyang \etal{}~\cite{ouyang2023llm} document non-determinism in code
generation, including that $T{=}0$ is not bit-deterministic on commercial
APIs; temperature changes output diversity~\cite{zhu2024hot}.
ReCode~\cite{wang2023recode} perturbs \emph{prompts} and asks whether pass
rate holds.
\sameeval{} leaves the prompt fixed and asks whether two answers are the same
program.
Studies of developer interaction~\cite{vaithilingam2022expectation,barke2023grounded}
treat a generation as a single artifact.

\paragraph{Clones and diffs.}
Fingerprint identity is closer to clone measurement than to
pass@$k$~\cite{roy2009clone}.
AST differencing~\cite{falleri2014gumtree} produces an edit script between two
trees; Zhang--Shasha tree-edit distance~\cite{zhang1989simple} is a related
numeric judge.
\sameeval{} asks how often independent regenerations land on the same
canonical form.
It is not a clone detector, not observational equivalence, and not algorithm
identity.
Clone and TED judges are not written into \sameat{}.

\paragraph{Repair versus overlay.}
Program repair with language models patches failing
code~\cite{xia2023apr}.
Copying the first passer, clustering by input--output behavior as in
AlphaCode~\cite{li2022alphacode}, execution-guided minimum Bayes-risk
selection~\cite{shi2022mbrexec}, and self-consistency-style
agreement~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency} are policies for what to keep.
None of them reports the pairwise form rate of the raw draws.
Consensus over execution behavior is known to track correctness
(AlphaCode clustering; execution-guided minimum Bayes
risk~\cite{shi2022mbrexec}).
We find that consensus over structural form, among programs that already
pass, does not.
Rewriting toward a stored verified form~\cite{roriz2026skytmsr} is likewise a
policy.
\sameeval{} is the overlay: same tasks, extra form reports.
This paper scores the overlay and then uses it to evaluate \skyt{} on
held-out generations,
including cache baselines that replay a stored passer instead of
regenerating.

\sameeval{} is not entropy, output variance, pass@$k$, pass@$1$, or
duplicate counting.
It estimates the probability of repeated agreement under a chosen
equivalence relation, with failures remaining in the \sameat{}
denominator.

\section{\sameeval{}}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Inputs and regenerations}
A \emph{task} supplies a prompt and a behavioral oracle (unit tests).
An experimental \emph{config} is one task $\times$ one model $\times$ one
temperature.
For each config we draw $N$ independent generations.
The \sameeval{} protocol uses $N{=}20$.
This study runs that protocol on all 164 HumanEval+ tasks
(Table~\ref{tab:he-table1}).

\subsection{Certification}
\label{sec:certification}
This paper distinguishes three test suites.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Base:} the original HumanEval unit tests.
  \item \textbf{Extra:} the additional EvalPlus tests.
  \item \textbf{Plus:} the full HumanEval+ suite (base and extra).
\end{itemize}

On the overlay, \emph{certified} means Plus tests pass.
Unparseable code is structurally invalid; it cannot certify, but it stays in
the \sameat{} denominator.
The headline evaluation applies \skyt{} to held-out test generations, using
only HumanEval base tests during repair (Section~\ref{sec:heldout-setup}).
Overlay \sameat{} and the headline held-out \sameat{} column remain
Plus-certified: the U-statistic can use tests \skyt{} did not see during
repair.

\subsection{Structural sameness}
\label{sec:sameness}
Two generations are the \emph{same form} iff both parse and their canonical-form
fingerprints match.
The canonical form is: parse to an AST; strip leading docstrings from every
module, function, and class body; $\alpha$-rename bound parameters and locals
when the task's naming policy is flexible (free names stay); unparse so the
tree and the source agree; hash \texttt{ast.dump} of that tree.

Distance 0 on the fingerprint is not semantic equivalence
(Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\subsection{Population measures}
\label{sec:measures}
Let a config have $N$ generations and write $C(N,2)$ for the unordered pairs.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{\sameat{}} (primary).
        The fraction of pairs in which both generations are certified and have
        the same form.
        \[
          \texttt{same@2}
          \;=\;
          \frac{\#\{\{i,j\}\,:\,\text{both certified and same form}\}}{C(N,2)}.
        \]
        This is a U-statistic of order 2.
        Failures remain in the denominator.
        It is defined on every config.
  \item \textbf{\sameatc{}} (secondary).
        The same identity check restricted to pairs of certified generations.
        Undefined when fewer than two generations certify; those configs are
        omitted from the mean of this column and the omission is reported.
\end{enumerate}

Always report \sameat{}, \sameatc{} (with the drop count), and pass rate
together, with $N$ and the task count in the same sentence as the headline.
Reporting \sameatc{} alone is forbidden: it rises when the pass rate falls.

Pairs within a config share generations and are not independent Bernoulli
trials.
Inference aggregates \emph{one score per task} and uses a
cluster bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples, seed 20260723).
We do not treat $C(N,2)$ pairs as binomial trials.

The shipped HumanEval \texttt{canonical\_solution} is an external baseline, not
a \sameeval{} canon.
The overlay itself does not pick a repair target.
Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes \skyt{} on held-out generations, scored on top
of the overlay.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Prompts are the 164 HumanEval tasks.
The overlay oracle is the full HumanEval+ suite~\cite{liu2023evalplus}
(Base plus Extra), not the original hidden HumanEval file alone.
Dataset pin: \texttt{evalplus==0.3.1}, HumanEvalPlus \texttt{v0.1.10},
\texttt{dataset\_md5 = 916d9bfe7b490c2447245ec91595fa4f}.
Table~\ref{tab:he-table1} reports all 164 tasks at protocol $N{=}20$.
The T-grid models are GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5
(\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929}), Claude Haiku 4.5
(\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5-20251001}), and GPT-6 Luna (\texttt{gpt-6-luna}),
each at $T\in\{0.0,0.7\}$ (26{,}240 generations / 1{,}312 configs).
$T{=}0$ is the lowest-variance setting a user can choose, and it is still not
deterministic; $T{=}0.7$ is a common sampling setting within the range used in
code-generation evaluation~\cite{chen2021evaluating}.
Claude Sonnet 5 (\texttt{claude-sonnet-5}) is scored at provider-default
sampling only (3{,}280 generations / 164 configs): Haiku and Luna take a
temperature; Luna uses reasoning effort \texttt{none}; Sonnet 5 exposes no
sampling parameters.
The Sonnet 5 row covers provider-default sampling, which users of recent
frontier models cannot change.
Expansion generations were produced with the Batch API (submitted 2026-09-24,
downloaded 2026-09-25); the original two models were synchronous.
Completions are stitched onto the prompt and scored in network-disabled Docker
(\texttt{python:3.13-slim@sha256:7e3a6aca9d74f93cca21a91d86a8dad8c34749afd5b4a98ee481c9c47b9f5ed4}).
Untrusted code is not executed on the host.
Overlay certified means Plus tests pass.
The human \texttt{canonical\_solution} is stored as an external baseline, not a
table column.

The overlay is scored with canonical-form fingerprints
(Section~\ref{sec:sameness}; \texttt{relation\_version}=2).

An exploratory analysis asks whether form diversity among Base-passing
programs carries information about Extra-test failure beyond the Base pass
rate.
That question is pre-registered on the original overlay; the expanded pool
in Section~\ref{sec:rq3} is a replication.
Forms are equivalence classes under the same fingerprint, computed only on
Base-passers.
The modal form of a program is the largest class among the other
Base-passers in its configuration (leave-one-out); ties are unclassified.
$D_c$ is one minus Base-certified \sameat{}.
$F_c$ is the Extra-failure rate among Base-passers.
Extra outcomes never define forms, modes, or $D_c$.
Configurations with fewer than two Base-passers are excluded.
The modal contrast also requires at least one modal and one non-modal
classified program.

Each reported interval resamples tasks with seed 20260723 (10{,}000 draws).
A call that uses that seed on the same task set draws the same tasks, so the
pooled estimate and the temperature interaction share those resamples.

\subsection{Held-out split}
\label{sec:heldout-setup}
\skyt{} selects a Certified Consensus form from certified train generations
and repairs later programs toward that stored form, rolling back if the
operational oracle fails.
If no train generation certifies, there is no canon and repair is skipped.
We do not change that pick rule.

On the frozen overlay we replay the policy without new language-model
calls.
For each of the 656 cells we draw 20 balanced 10/10 splits (seed 20260723).
Certified Consensus is selected on the train 10 only.
The transformer repairs the test 10 toward that form.
\sameat{} and \sameatc{} are U-statistics on the test 10
($C(10,2){=}45$ pairs) and then averaged over the 20 splits.
Paired treatment effects $\Delta{=}\mathrm{post}{-}\mathrm{pre}$ use a
cluster bootstrap by \texttt{task\_id} (10{,}000, seed 20260723), preserving
the pre/post pairing.
\sameat{} is the primary repeatability metric because it is defined on every
config.
\sameatc{} is diagnostic; we report it on the intersection of configs
eligible both before and after (common support) and we report coverage.

The headline evaluation is \skyt{} on held-out test generations, using only
HumanEval base tests during repair and reserving HumanEval+ extra tests for
final evaluation.
Extra tests do not select the canon and do not authorize a rollback.
A robustness run (Section~\ref{sec:rq4-robustness}) applies \skyt{} to the
same test 10 using the full Plus suite for pick, rollback, and scoring.
Post scores live in sibling trees;
\texttt{outputs/benchmark/humaneval\_plus\_164\_n20/} is not overwritten.

\subsection{RQ1: Correctness does not imply repeatability}
\label{sec:rq1}

protocol $N{=}20$.
Aggregation is a task-mean with cluster bootstrap by \texttt{task\_id}

\subsection{RQ2: Validity of the measure}
\label{sec:rq2}
Human agreement with the fingerprint is pending; a 128-pair dual-annotator
protocol is prepared (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

\paragraph{Computational rulers.}
The overlay \sameat{} gap is not an artifact of one fingerprint.
A stricter parse-and-unparse identity yields 59.8\% (55.1--64.4);
AST identity after docstring stripping yields 61.9\% (57.1--66.5);
the production SameEval fingerprint ($\alpha$-rename) is 65.9\% (61.0--70.6).
Tighter rulers widen the overlay gap.
The Pre and post columns of Table~\ref{tab:fingerprint} are the held-out
mitigation comparison and are read with Section~\ref{sec:rq4}.

\paragraph{Rank comparison against pass rate.}
At each temperature we compare every T-grid model pair on Plus pass and on
\sameatc{} (common support), with paired task-cluster CIs.
A rank reversal is claimed only when the \sameatc{} CI excludes zero with
sign opposite to the Plus-pass difference.
At $T{=}0.0$, GPT-6 Luna outranks GPT-4o-mini on Plus pass
($+10.2$\,pp [4.5, 16.2]) while GPT-4o-mini outranks Luna on \sameatc{}
($+12.3$\,pp [7.3, 17.3]): the two measures reverse those two models.
Luna similarly reverses against Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 4.5 on \sameatc{} even
when the Plus-pass differences include zero.
At $T{=}0.7$, Haiku 4.5 outranks Sonnet 4.5 on Plus pass in point estimate
while Sonnet 4.5 outranks Haiku on \sameatc{}
($-9.0$\,pp [$-14.9$, $-3.2$] for Haiku minus Sonnet).

\paragraph{Sample-size reliability.}
Subsampling the first $N\in\{4,6,10,14\}$ stored generations per config,
the task-cluster CI width for \sameat{} shrinks slowly and the estimate

\subsection{RQ3: Repeatability and hidden-test correctness}
\label{sec:rq3}
Both analyses below were pre-registered on the original overlay and are
exploratory.
The expanded pool is a replication: GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude
Haiku 4.5, and GPT-6 Luna at $T\in\{0.0,0.7\}$, plus Claude Sonnet 5 at
provider-default sampling (1{,}476 configs).
Sonnet 5 is inside that pool, not a separate diversity row.
The uniformity check is post hoc and descriptive.

On the pre-registered overlay, form diversity carried no detectable signal
about hidden-test failure beyond the pass rate.
Among Base-passing programs, modal and non-modal forms survived Extra tests
at the same rate ($\Delta = 0.06$\,pp [$-1.4$, $1.4$]).
Configurations with more diverse forms had slightly higher Extra-failure
rates ($\rho = 0.13$ [0.02, 0.23]), but the association did not survive
adjustment for Base pass rate (partial $\rho = 0.07$ [$-0.04$, 0.18]) or
for task (within-task $\rho = 0.02$ [$-0.10$, 0.13]).

In the expanded pool, the association survives adjustment for Base pass rate
(partial $\rho = 0.13$ [0.06, 0.20]) but not for task (within-task
$\rho = 0.04$ [$-0.02$, 0.11]).
Modal and non-modal forms again survived Extra tests at similar rates
($\Delta = -0.01$\,pp [$-1.4$, $1.5$]).
The association is therefore between tasks: tasks whose passing programs
take many forms also tend to fail Extra tests, but within a task, more
diverse configurations are not detectably more error-prone.
Task-level factors such as specification ambiguity or difficulty may drive
both.

On the pre-registered overlay, configurations whose Base-passing programs
all shared one form still failed Extra tests 10.7\% of the time: repeatable
generation can be repeatably wrong.
In a post hoc check, when any Base-passing program failed Extra tests, all
of them failed in 65.0\% [50.0, 80.3] of configurations (expanded pool:
53.0\% [39.6, 66.7]).
Hidden-test failures were often shared across forms, consistent with a
common misreading of the specification rather than the quirks of one form,
which is why form diversity does not signal them within a task.

Repeatability and hidden-test correctness are distinct properties, which is
why \sameeval{} measures the former separately.

\subsection{RQ4: Evaluating mitigation policies}
\label{sec:rq4}
RQ4 uses the two original models (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5); the
expansion models are scored on the overlay only.
RQ4 uses \sameeval{} to evaluate policies, not to promote one.
On static prompts, replaying a stored program is the ceiling; \skyt{} is
evaluated as a policy that still regenerates.

\paragraph{Policies and design.}
\label{sec:rq4-policies}
The policies are raw generation, first Base-certified cache, Base Consensus
cache, and \skyt{}.
Each sees only HumanEval base tests at selection time.
Extra tests are reserved for evaluation.

\paragraph{Repeatability and correctness under each policy.}
On the same 13{,}120 stored programs we apply \skyt{} to held-out test
generations, using only HumanEval base tests during repair and reserving
HumanEval+ extra tests for final evaluation.
Plus \sameat{} (Plus-certification in the U-statistic) rises from 66.1\% to
83.1\%, a 17.0 percentage-point increase (95\% CI 14.5--19.6).
Plus pass changes from 83.3\% to 85.9\% ($+$2.7\,pp, 1.7--3.8) and does not
deteriorate.
The 66.1\% pre-\sameat{} differs from the RQ4-subset row of
Table~\ref{tab:he-table1} (65.9\%)
because those scores are on the held-out generations, not all 20 draws; it is not a
new overlay finding.
The repeatability effect therefore persists under stronger tests unavailable
to \skyt{} during repair.
That is not a cross-benchmark generalization claim.

Figure~\ref{fig:arrows} shows the four cells in Plus-pass $\times$ \sameat{}
space: arrows are mostly vertical.
GPT-4o-mini at $T{=}0.7$ is the exception that also moves right
($+$5.9\,pp Plus pass, 3.5--8.5).

\paragraph{Temperature.}
\label{sec:rq4-temperature}
The primary explanatory result is the paired temperature interaction
$\Delta_{T{=}0.7}-\Delta_{T{=}0.0}$ on the same oracle-split tree as RQ2
and RQ3.
It is $+18.0$\,pp (95\% CI 15.0--21.1) pooled across models
($+$23.4\,pp for GPT-4o-mini, $+12.6$\,pp for Claude).
The Plus-operational robustness interaction is $+18.1$\,pp (15.0--21.2).
Cell lifts on Table~\ref{tab:heldout} are
$+$29.3\,pp versus $+5.9$\,pp for GPT-4o-mini and
$+$22.8\,pp versus $+10.1$\,pp for Claude Sonnet 4.5.
The benefit is larger at $T{=}0.7$ than at $T{=}0.0$ ($+18.0$\,pp); with two
settings we do not estimate a dose--response curve.

Figure~\ref{fig:lift} shows the ceiling: many cells are rewritten onto a
single form (lift $\approx 100{-}$baseline).
We do not treat mechanically related quantities---whether the transformer
fired, train modal share among already-certified programs---as independent
discoveries.

Mean rollback per config is 0 when \skyt{} on the held-out generations also sees Plus
tests during repair.
The dominant observed source of zero repeatability gain is the absence of a
certified train consensus (87/656 configurations; 36 tasks have at least one
such cell).
When Extra tests are reserved for final evaluation, four configurations
exhibit held-out Plus correctness regressions, indicating that operational
certification does not fully eliminate repair risk.
The unseen Extra tests can detect regressions unavailable to \skyt{}, which
confirms that the held-out tests are genuinely informative.
Overall Plus correctness still improves ($+$2.7\,pp, 1.7--3.8).
Python construct rates (loops, recursion, nested defs) do not isolate the
wins; list comprehensions are more common among no-canon cells (35\% of
generations) than among the ten largest lifts (0\%).
Largest lifts include HumanEval/83 (\texttt{starts\_one\_ends}) under
GPT-4o-mini, where pre \sameat{} is 0--1\% and \skyt{} rewrites the test slice
onto the train consensus (post \sameat{} $1.0$; Plus-pass $29\%\rightarrow 100\%$
at $T{=}0.7$).
HumanEval/109 under Claude at $T{=}0.7$ already has Plus-pass $100\%$ before
\skyt{}; \sameat{} still rises from $4.9\%$ to $100\%$.
\skyt{} is most useful when instability is already measurable.



\paragraph{Where the effect comes from.}
\label{sec:rq4-mechanism}
When \skyt{} fires, the typical rewrite is Level-3 substitution of the
entry-point AST with the train Certified Consensus function.
The live transformer (agents off) has no separate Level-1 style pass:
disabling Level 2 and Level 3 is identical to Raw (Table~\ref{tab:level-ablation}).
Level 2 is oracle-gated statement removal.
Isolating it (same transformer, \texttt{max\_transformation\_level=2},
sibling Docker tree
\texttt{outputs/benchmark/humaneval\_plus\_164\_n20\_skyt\_heldout\_oracle\_split\_l2})
moves Plus \sameat{} from 66.1\% to 66.4\%
($+$0.3\,pp, 0.1--0.5) and leaves Plus pass at 83.3\%.
It transforms 0.04 of 10 test programs per split.
Statement removal does not account for the live \skyt{} repeatability lift.
Full live \skyt{} (Level 2 then Level 3) is Table~\ref{tab:heldout}:
Plus \sameat{} 66.1\%$\rightarrow$83.1\% ($+$17.0\,pp, 14.5--19.6),
transforming 1.7 of 10 test programs per split on average.
A no-Docker Level-3-only inherit replay substitutes the entry-point AST and
inherits the train consensus oracle, without Level-2 simplification and
without live rollback.
That sibling tree reaches Plus \sameat{} 66.1\%$\rightarrow$84.2\%
($+$18.1\,pp, 15.5--20.8) and Plus pass 83.3\%$\rightarrow$85.2\%,
but it transforms 9.5 of 10 test programs per split---more aggressive than
live \skyt{}.
We do not treat inherit as a strict subset of the headline pipeline.
The honest reading is that canonical substitution can produce a
repeatability lift at least as large as live \skyt{}; live \skyt{} is the
more conservative policy.
Level 3 does not invent an algorithm from failing fragments: it pastes a
stored verified function into the test program.
Inspection of the largest lifts matches that mechanism: pre-\skyt{} passers
already disagree on counting or control-flow shape, and post \sameat{} of
$1.0$ means the test slice landed on the train form.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

HumanEval+ functional correctness and generation repeatability diverge.
Passing plus tests is not equivalent to repeatable generation.
\sameat{} exposes a gap that pass rate alone does not show.

The two shortcuts in Section~\ref{sec:motivation} remain available after
Table~\ref{tab:he-table1}.
Copy-first would freeze, for GPT-4o-mini at $T{=}0.7$, a passer from a cell
whose certified pairs agree only $60.0\%$ of the time on the overlay.
Stop-after-first-pass would report the $79.7\%$ plus-pass and never the
$48.9\%$ \sameat{}.

That is expected: caching eliminates regeneration.
\skyt{} is the policy that still regenerates, then constrains the artifact
toward a frozen form and rolls back if the operational oracle fails.
We do not claim that this HumanEval+ replay proves \skyt{} is preferable to
caching when regeneration is optional.
Static prompts favor cache-based policies.
\skyt{} is aimed at settings where regeneration remains necessary and replay
is unavailable or unsafe---changed context, changed requirements, incremental
edits, model updates, or a policy that forbids replaying a stored file.
Those settings are not this experiment.

Relaxing the operational oracle from HumanEval+ to original HumanEval
increases canon availability from 569/656 to 627/656 configurations, while
held-out Plus repeatability remains essentially unchanged
(Table~\ref{tab:heldout} versus Table~\ref{tab:plus-operational}).
How often a train canon exists therefore depends on operational-oracle
strictness and warrants further study; we do not treat that coverage shift
as a causal claim beyond this design.

Diversity in the sample is what \sameat{} measures.
Diversity in what is \emph{shipped} is still a re-read for no behavioral
gain.
This paper does not merge useful fragments from failing programs and does
not claim semantic equivalence.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On HumanEval+, Plus pass and \sameat{} remain far apart on the T-grid pool:
86.4\% versus 67.7\%, and the gap is widest at $T{=}0.7$
(GPT-4o-mini: 79.7\% versus 48.9\%).
Three computational rulers preserve that gap; \sameatc{} can reverse model
order relative to Plus pass, and $N{=}10$ already tracks $N{=}20$ closely.
We found no evidence that, within a task, form diversity signals
hidden-test failure (exploratory).
The expanded-pool replication is reported in Section~\ref{sec:rq3}.
\sameeval{} separates mitigation policies on the two original models' held-out
generations: a stored program is the ceiling on static prompts, and the
\skyt{} lift comes from canonical substitution rather than statement removal.

\sameeval{} leaves one question open by design.
When regeneration is required---changed context, incremental edits, model
updates---does constraining form toward a verified canon beat replaying a
stored program?
Answering it requires a regeneration track for \sameeval{}: the same tasks
under perturbed contexts, where a stored program may no longer apply.
We leave both the track and the comparison to future work.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Construct.}
Fingerprint identity is the operational definition of ``same form.''
It is not observational equivalence, algorithm identity, or semantic
equivalence.
Human agreement with the fingerprint is not yet in this paper; a
128-pair dual-annotator protocol (two independent blind sheets; annotators
do not see model, temperature, or the fingerprint verdict) is prepared.
Three computational rulers (normalized source, AST, SameEval v2) preserve
the overlay gap and the held-out SKYT lift; SameEval is the most lenient
of the three.
Post-SKYT programs in the persist replay are scored under all three rulers
(Table~\ref{tab:fingerprint}).
\sameatc{} depends on the certification definition and on which configs
have at least two passers; we report coverage and common support.
The extractor does not transfer to multi-file
SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench}.

\paragraph{Internal.}
The 10/10 split prevents selecting the canon from the evaluation
generations, but it does not by itself provide an independent correctness
oracle.
The headline evaluation applies \skyt{} to held-out test generations, using
only HumanEval base tests during repair and reserving HumanEval+ extra tests
for final evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:heldout}; 0 new LLM calls).
Repair may still use the operational (Base) oracle by design.
Plus pass includes Base tests, so the Plus-pass column is not a fully unseen
correctness metric; Extra tests are unseen.
Table~\ref{tab:extra-survival} reports Extra-test survival among
Base-certified generations.
RQ2, RQ3, and RQ4 use the same oracle-split tree; Plus-operational results
are robustness (Table~\ref{tab:plus-operational}).
A robustness run applies \skyt{} to the same held-out generations using the full Plus
suite for pick, rollback, and scoring (Table~\ref{tab:plus-operational}).
Plus \sameat{} lift matches to the reported precision.
Using only the first $B\in\{5,10,15,20\}$ stored splits, the same@2 lift
stays 17.0\,pp (CIs 14.4--19.7).
That is prefix robustness on the same 20 splits.
A nested persist replay of $B\in\{20,50,100,500\}$ from the same seed
(Table~\ref{tab:nested-b}) moves the lift from $+17.1$ to $+17.3$\,pp;
$B{=}500$ equals $B{=}100$ at this precision.

\paragraph{External.}
HumanEval+ only; Python only; five closed models from two API vendors
(no open-weight model); two temperatures plus provider-default sampling for
one model; no dose--response claim; function-level tasks.
RQ4 is evaluated on two models; replicating the policy comparison on the
expansion models is future work.
No repository-level change, no embedded execution environment, and no
safety-certification study.
We do not claim that these results demonstrate readiness for
safety-critical generation.

\paragraph{Baseline.}
Static prompts permit caching.
Cache is a legitimate baseline.
\skyt{}'s advantage must not be framed as beating caching unless the
numbers show it, and even then only for the regeneration setting this
design actually tests.

\paragraph{Exploratory diversity signal.}
The modal-form and diversity analyses were specified before computation,
after a related count of distinct forms had already been seen in the
\skyt{} lift results.
\texttt{same()} is structural, not semantic.
Extra tests are incomplete, so Extra survival is a proxy for hidden
correctness.
$F_c$ is zero for many configurations; the association uses rank correlation
for that reason.
On the pre-registered overlay, A1 is estimable only for configurations with
at least two passing forms (226 of 656); it says nothing about
configurations with a single passing form.
The uniformity check is post hoc.
The expanded-pool replication pools models and has about twice the
configurations of the original analysis; its partial correlation excludes
zero while its within-task correlation does not.
We read the difference as a small between-task association, not as a
within-task signal.

\paragraph{Statistical conclusion.}
Inference is a cluster bootstrap by task, not Wilson on $C(N,2)$ pairs.
Treatment-effect CIs are paired.
Conditional-metric support is reported.
Model$\times$temperature cells share tasks; per-cell intervals should
not be read as independent populations.

\paragraph{Two pairwise columns.}
\sameat{} keeps failures in the denominator.
\sameatc{} drops configs with fewer than two passers and therefore conditions
on tasks the model can already pass.
This paper reports both, with the pass rate.
\sameat{} is a U-statistic of order 2, not a squared pass rate.
We do not treat $p^2$ as a null, and we do not headline pass minus \sameat{}
as the form gap.

\paragraph{Sample size and oracle suite.}
Protocol $N{=}20$ on all 164 tasks.
Certified on the overlay inherits the Plus suite.
Plus tests are finite.
HumanEval/130 plus inputs with huge integers were capped; the worker uses a
per-case time limit.

\paragraph{Contamination and models.}
HumanEval prompts are likely in pretraining.
$T{=}0$ is not bit-deterministic; draws are a timestamped snapshot.
